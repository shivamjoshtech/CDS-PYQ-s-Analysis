# Part 0 — How to Read This Document

This document merges the complete topic-wise analysis of all six CDS papers you uploaded — CDS I 2024, CDS I 2025, CDS I 2026, CDS II 2023, CDS II 2024, and CDS II 2025 — into one master reference. Nothing from any of the six source files has been left out: every topic, sub-topic, and question-count has been carried forward, cross-matched across papers, and combined into the priority rankings below.

Priority tiers used throughout this document:

- 🔴 **Tier 1 — Highest Priority**: appears heavily in nearly every paper; master this first.
- 🟠 **Tier 2 — High Priority**: substantial and consistent weight, but slightly behind Tier 1.
- 🟡 **Tier 3 — Medium Priority**: present in most papers but at moderate weight.
- 🟢 **Tier 4 — Lower Priority**: smaller or occasional, but should not be skipped entirely.

The document is organised in five parts: Part 1 (General Knowledge), Part 2 (Elementary Mathematics), Part 3 (English) each give the per-paper question counts, the merged cross-paper topic table, and a detailed sub-topic breakdown — **including the Cross-Paper Pattern column, which explains how each sub-topic behaves across all 6 papers (consistency, GK↔English overlap, guaranteed formats, rising/falling trend, and the high-ROI study strategy for that sub-topic), not just its raw count.** Part 4 gives cross-paper strategic insights common to all three sections. Part 5 is the final consolidated, priority-ranked study order across all three papers combined.

## 2.3 — Detailed Sub-Topic Breakdown by Maths Topic (with Cross-Paper Pattern)

### Geometry (Triangles, Circles, Quadrilaterals, Mensuration-adjacent) — 🔴 Tier 1 — Highest

*The single biggest Maths topic in 4 of the 6 papers, ranging 16–23 Qs (avg ~19.5/paper, ~19.5% of the paper). CDS I 2024, CDS I 2026, and CDS II 2025 each call Geometry their LARGEST single topic.*

| Sub-topic | What's Tested (Merged Across Papers) | Cross-Paper Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Triangles | Area/altitude relations, Heron's formula, angle bisector theorem (BD/DC = AB/AC), medians (sum of squares), incircle/circumcircle, similarity, right-triangle properties, equilateral-triangle tests. | Most repeated theorem across papers: the Angle Bisector Theorem and Heron's formula. |
| Circles | Chord properties (AE×EB), tangent properties (internal/external), two-circles-touching, inscribed/circumscribed circles, sector & segment ratios, concentric circles, cyclic quadrilaterals. | A second, equally large cluster — chord-tangent theorems recur every single paper. |
| Quadrilaterals & Polygons | Diagonal-based area, rectangle vs parallelogram comparisons, trapeziums, midpoint quadrilaterals (Varignon-type). | Moderate but steady, 4–6 Qs/paper. |
| Linked Figure-Sets ('Comprehension' geometry) | Many CDS Geometry questions come in linked 2–4 question sets sharing one diagram (e.g. two circles in a rectangle, triangle with incircle + tangent circles, square with inscribed triangle). | High-ROI strategy: solving the base figure correctly unlocks multiple marks at once — practice by figure archetype, not just by formula. |

### Algebra (incl. Quadratics, Polynomials, Surds, Number Theory crossover) — 🔴 Tier 1 — Highest

*Consistently 9–20 Qs/paper (avg ~15.0). Quadratic-equation root relationships (sum=−b/a, product=c/a) and polynomial HCF/factorization are near-guaranteed every cycle.*

|---|---|---|

| Surds, Indices & Ratio-Algebra | Surd simplification (comprehension-style: x=(√a+√b)/(√a−√b) type), ratio-proportion algebra, variation (direct/inverse). | Recurs across most papers, moderate weight. |
| Sequences (AP/GP) | Sum of AP, maximum-sum problems, continued fractions/series. | Smaller, but a fixed recurring sub-block. |

### Data Sufficiency (fixed block) — 🔴 Tier 1 — Highest

*Where explicitly tagged (4 of 6 papers, always exactly 10 Qs), this is described in EVERY paper's own analysis as a '100% guaranteed, fixed-format block.' In the other two papers it is folded into Statistics/other but the same logic-block format applies. This is the single most predictable 10 marks in the whole Maths paper.*

| Sub-topic | What's Tested (Merged Across Papers) | Cross-Paper Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Statement-Sufficiency Logic | 4-option format: (a) one statement alone sufficient, (b) either statement alone sufficient, (c) both together only, (d) not answerable even together. | Master this logic ONCE — the format never changes, only the underlying numeric/geometric content does. |
| Recurring Content Types | Number properties, geometry sufficiency (e.g. 'is the triangle equilateral'), ages/weights, equations, interest problems. | High-certainty, low-effort marks once the format is internalised. |

# Part 4 — Cross-Paper Strategic Insights (All 6 Papers, All 3 Sections)

These insights were independently observed in multiple individual paper analyses and are confirmed here by looking across all six papers together.

## 4.1 — The Single Biggest Insight: GK ↔ English Synergy

Every one of the six papers, independently, makes the same observation: English's sentence-ordering, para-jumble, and cloze-test passages are built from the same content pool as GK's History, Geography, Economy, Polity, and Current Affairs sections — Industrial Revolution, Green Revolution, NEP 2020, RCEP/SCO/BRICS, Ujjwala Yojana, Rozgar Mela, droughts, glaciation, the Great Depression, French/Russian Revolutions, and dozens more recur as both direct GK questions AND as English passage material.

- Studying Current Affairs and NCERT History/Geography/Polity/Economy has DOUBLE ROI — it directly raises your GK score AND speeds up reading comprehension in English's jumble/cloze sections (roughly 20–25 questions per paper).
- Conversely, a candidate who is grammatically strong but weak in GS will still lose time on jumbles/cloze simply from not recognising the underlying content quickly.
- Recommendation: treat GS reading (NCERT, PIB releases, The Hindu/editorial-style English) as a single dual-purpose activity covering both papers at once.

## 4.2 — Data Sufficiency (Maths) Is the Most Predictable Block in the Entire Exam

Every paper that explicitly tags it confirms the exact same finding: a fixed 10-question block (commonly Q61–70 or Q91–100 depending on the paper) tests Statement I/II sufficiency using the identical 4-option logic — (a) one statement alone, (b) either statement alone, (c) both together only, (d) not answerable even together. The underlying content rotates (number properties, geometry, ages/weights, interest) but the reasoning skill is constant.

- This is the highest-certainty, lowest-additional-effort 10 marks in the whole Maths paper once the logic format is mastered.

## 4.3 — Geometry Dominates Mathematics, and Comes in Linked Figure-Sets

In 4 of 6 papers, Geometry is explicitly called the single largest Maths topic (16–23 Qs). Multiple papers independently note that many Geometry questions arrive as linked 2–4 question sets sharing one diagram (two circles in a rectangle; triangle with incircle plus tangent circles; square with inscribed triangle).

- Mastering one figure archetype unlocks multiple questions at once — practice by figure-type, not only by formula.

## 4.4 — GK's Current Affairs Is the Largest and Fastest-Growing Section

Across the six papers, Current Affairs (including Defence & Government Schemes) ranges from 17 to 32 questions and averages roughly 24 per paper — larger than History or Geography individually in most years. Static GK is shrinking; dynamic, last-12-months GK is expanding.

- Defence & Military Current Affairs (DRDO launches, Army/Navy operations, new equipment, bilateral exercises, training-institution facts) is a consistently heavy, CDS-specific sub-cluster — this deserves a dedicated running tracker every cycle, since it is directly aligned with the CDS candidate profile and is under-covered in generic GK books.

## 4.5 — History + Economy + Current Affairs + Geography + Polity = the Core of GK

These five subjects together account for roughly 79% of the entire GK paper across all six papers combined (History ~20.3 avg + Geography ~18.8 avg + Polity ~13.0 avg + Economy ~12.8 avg + Current Affairs ~23.8 avg, out of 120). The remaining science subjects (Physics, Chemistry, Biology) are individually stable at ~9–10 each but collectively smaller than any one of the core five.

## 4.6 — Grammar + Vocabulary Together Decide Most of the English Score

Across all six papers, Grammar (avg ~35.8/paper) and Vocabulary (avg ~36.7/paper) combined make up roughly 60% of the English paper on their own, and together with Sentence Ordering (avg ~20/paper) account for over 90%. Reading Comprehension and Cloze, while present, are comparatively smaller and less consistent (appearing as dedicated sections in only 3–4 of the 6 papers).

## 4.7 — Polity Has Shifted From Generic Civics to Specific Articles & Schedules

Across multiple recent papers, Polity questions increasingly cite exact Article numbers, Schedule numbers, and Amendment numbers (Article 371-series, 7th/8th Schedule, Part IV-A, 86th/101st/106th Amendments) rather than asking about the general structure of government. Basic civics knowledge alone is no longer sufficient.

## 4.8 — Economy Questions Are Increasingly Numerical/Applied

Several recent papers note a shift toward applied calculation (inflation from price-index data, fiscal-multiplier logic, GDP-deflator computation) rather than pure definitional recall. Pure theory revision should be paired with worked numerical practice.

## 4.9 — Negative Marking Is Identical and Consistent Across All Six Papers

Every paper in every section applies a 1/3rd-mark penalty for a wrong answer, with zero penalty for an unattempted question. The consistent strategic advice across all six analyses: avoid blind guessing, but attempt confidently once you have eliminated down to two options.

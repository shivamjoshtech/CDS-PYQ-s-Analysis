## 3.3 Detailed Topic-Wise Breakdown

### A. Vocabulary — Synonym/Contextual Substitution (Q1–10, 10 Qs)
Words tested: *bilateral, indefinite, antipathy, diligent, impassioned, postscript, simulism, paternal, reflective, salutary.* High-register, literary/academic vocabulary consistently tested.

### B. Spotting Errors (Q11–20, 10 Qs)
Subject-verb agreement, article usage, word choice, redundancy, idiomatic phrasing. High proportion of "No Error" correct answers (Q12,13,15,17,18,19,20) — candidates must resist over-correcting.

### C. Prepositions/Determiners (Q21–25, 5 Qs)
Distributive determiners (each/every/all/any), quantifiers (further/several/most), prepositions with verbs, relative determiners, correlative conjunctions.

### D. Confusing/Similar Sounding Words (Q26–30, 5 Qs)
Word triplets: Dairy/Dreary/Diary, Braid/Beard/Brood, Lesion/Legion/Lesson, Depose/Deplore/Deport, Bate/Bait/Bade. Classic homophone confusion testing.

### E. Idioms and Proverbs (Q31–40, 10 Qs)
Idioms: *ears are flapping, twist of the knife, blow your own trumpet, get under someone's skin, make heavy weather, sweep under the carpet, a purple patch, put out to pasture.* Proverbs: *Many hands make light work, A bird in hand...* Mix of classic + modern sports/business idioms.

### F. Sentence Co-relationship (Q41–50, 10 Qs)
S1–S2 logic: contradicts/confirms/corroborates/contrasts/extends/explains. Content vehicles: failure/success, Big Bang, climate science, bottled water, sports popularity, forest cover, Indian diaspora. Skill tested is logical reasoning, content is incidental.

### G. Sentence Jumbles (Q51–60, 10 Qs)
Content heavily drawn from **govt schemes**: Rozgar Mela/NSDC, Ujjwala Yojana 2.0, MSME classification, entrepreneurship policy, pollution control, EIA, women-led development, intangible heritage, OTT regulation. Overlaps with GS/PIB content.

### H. Para Jumbles (Q61–70, 10 Qs)
Content: Tungabhadra river, glaciation, Russian Revolution 1917, Indian freedom struggle, French Revolution/Louis XVI, Montesquieu, insolation/solar energy, evolution/geological periods. Strong overlap with GS topics (History, Geography, Science).

### I. Parts of Speech — List Matching (Q71–80, 10 Qs)
Word-family transformation: qualify→qualification/conditionally/qualifying; err→error/erroneously/erroneous. Roots: qualify, err, care, duple, emanate/exodus, spectate, know, cogitate, intuit, hear.

### J. Parts of Speech — Identify (Q81–85, 5 Qs)
Identify grammatical category (collective noun, abstract noun, distributive adjective, plural noun, adverb) of underlined word.

### K. Discourse Markers (Q86–90, 5 Qs)
therefore/thus/for example, somewhat/indeed/fortunately, in particular/matter of fact, significantly/unexpectedly, finally/fatally. Academic/argumentative essay register.

### L. Word Pairs — Confusable (Q91–95, 5 Qs)
Lose/Loose, Anyway/Any way, Allusion/Illusion, Appraise/Apprise, Climactic/Climatic.

### M. Correct Usage (Q96–100, 5 Qs)
Advice/Advise, Illicit/Elicit, Effect/Affect, Brake/Break, Incite/Insight — homophones that are grammatically different parts of speech.

### N. Vocabulary — List Matching (Q101–110, 10 Qs)
Advanced words: *Ensue, Morass, Imbroglio, Potpourri, Malversation, Conundrum, Incessant, Mulct, Impugn, Spew, Harangue, Gibberish, Caveat, Amble, Acolyte, Archaic, Kernel, Brook, Jinx, Solace, Feat, Fate, Fathom, Faux pas, Semitic, Seminary, Send off, Semblance, Hatch, Haughty, Gruff, Gruelling, Extirpate, Extol, Extremity, Expunge, Devious, Devolution, Detriment, Detract.* Densest pure-vocabulary section.

### O. Cloze Test (Q111–120, 10 Qs)
One passage on **education inequality/learning outcomes across countries** (World Bank/UNESCO-style). Tests: passive voice, modals, prepositions, word forms, demonstrative pronouns, conjunctions, verb forms, noun forms, quantifiers.

# 🧠 PART 5: KEY STRATEGIC OBSERVATIONS (Combined, My Own Analysis)

## General Knowledge
- **Current Affairs is now the single largest GK section**, bigger than History or Polity individually — static GK is shrinking, dynamic GK is expanding. Follow CA for at least 12 months prior to exam.
- **Defence & Military CA is a near-guaranteed dedicated cluster** (8+ Qs) — being a defence services exam, expect detailed coverage of military operations, naval ships/equipment, missile systems (BrahMos, SCALP, HAMMER), bilateral exercises.
- **History is Modern-India-heavy** — nationalist movements, social reformers, women freedom fighters, freedom-struggle newspapers/journals repeat every year.
- **Polity is "Schedule & Article" centric** — Articles 370/371-series, 7th & 8th Schedule, Part IV-A repeat across papers.
- **Geography leans into Climatology/World Physical Geography** (NCERT Class 11 level) more than just Indian geography.
- **Science sections are NCERT conceptual, not numerical-heavy** — definition/property/identification MCQs.
- **Computer Science is small, stable, learnable** — binary systems, networking, logic gates repeat predictably.

## Elementary Mathematics
- **Geometry (Triangles + Circles) is the biggest scoring opportunity** — theorem-based (angle bisector theorem, tangent properties, area ratios), not just formula plug-in.
- **Statistics is "free 15 marks" if practiced** — always grouped passage-sets at paper's end; tests calculation speed and careful reading, not new concepts.
- **Mensuration questions are paired/grouped passages** (one scenario → 2 questions) — frustums, spheres-cut-into-pots, rainfall-volume, box-weight are stock formats.
- **Algebra is Quadratic-Equation-heavy** — common roots, sum/product of roots, polynomial HCF/factorization repeat almost every paper. High-yield if formulas memorized (sum=−b/a, product=c/a).
- **Trigonometry is identity-manipulation heavy**, not just height-distance — expect algebraic-style trig simplification.
- **Number Theory is small but "trick-based"** — modular arithmetic, remainder theorem patterns secure quick marks.

## English
- **Vocabulary is king** — ~40/120 (33%) directly/indirectly test word meaning. Build advanced vocabulary bank (*malversation, palimpsest, imbroglio, conundrum, extirpate*).
- **Jumbles are heavily GS-flavored** — sentence/para jumbles draw on Indian govt schemes (Ujjwala, MSME, Rozgar Mela), History (French/Russian Revolution, freedom struggle), Physical Geography/Science (glaciation, insolation, climate). **Reading GS content pays double dividends — for the GK paper AND for English jumble comprehension speed.**
- **Word-family transformation (Parts of Speech matching) is a guaranteed fixture** — 10 Qs every paper on noun/verb/adverb/adjective forms of same root.
- **"No Error" is a real, frequent answer** in error-spotting — don't over-hunt for mistakes that aren't there.
- **Commonly confused word pairs recur predictably**: advice/advise, effect/affect, lose/loose, illicit/elicit, brake/break — a focused 50-pair confusable-words list covers a lot.
- **Cloze passages are thematically "serious/policy" content** (education inequality, global development) — practice cloze from editorial/policy English (The Hindu editorials etc.), not casual narrative text.
- **Idioms include classical proverbs AND modern/sports/business idioms** ("purple patch," "put out to pasture") — a pure old-proverb list isn't enough.
- **Discourse markers + modals + cloze sections** suggest CDS tests **formal/academic register English**, not conversational English.

## 🔗 Cross-Paper Insight (Important!)
There is a **direct overlap between English jumbles/cloze content and GS topics** (govt schemes, history, geography, science). This means:
- Studying GS thoroughly doesn't just help the GK paper — it **speeds up reading comprehension and content-familiarity in the English paper's jumble/cloze sections too.**
- Conversely, candidates who are weak in GS but strong in pure grammar may still slow down on jumbles/cloze because they don't recognize the underlying content (e.g., not knowing what "Rozgar Mela" or "insolation" refers to costs reading-comprehension time, even if grammar skill is fine).
- **Recommendation: Integrate GS reading (PIB releases, NCERT Geography/History, The Hindu editorials) into English prep as a dual-purpose activity.**

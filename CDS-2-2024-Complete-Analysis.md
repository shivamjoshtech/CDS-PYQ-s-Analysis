## A.3 Key Observations — Maths

- **Geometry + Mensuration (~27%)** is the single biggest chunk — triangles (Heron's formula, altitude-based area), circles (tangents, chords, common tangent circles), 3D solids (cone/sphere/cylinder volume conversion) dominate.
- **Number System (21%)** is very heavy — remainder theorems, HCF-LCM with constraints, digit-sum divisibility (9, 3), "find x such that expression divisible by..." type repeated multiple times (Q3, Q25, Q26, Q45, Q75, Q76, Q89, Q97).
- **Trigonometry (16%)** mostly identity-proving/value-finding (formula-recall based), with limited heights-and-distances (Q18, Q46).
- **Data Sufficiency (Q61–70, fixed 10 Qs)** — now a guaranteed, predictable block every paper. Tests whether Statement I/II alone or together answer a numeric question (ages, work, weights, equations, interest).
- **Statistics reliably ~6%** every paper — mean/median/mode + grouped frequency distribution.
- **Time-Speed-Distance/Work is unusually low (only 3 Qs)** this attempt — historically a bigger chunk in older CDS papers.

## B.3 Detailed Sub-topic Breakdown — GK

**Biology (9 Qs):** Plant physiology dominates — micronutrients, xylem/phloem, plant hormones (auxin/cytokinin/ABA/gibberellin), starch/biomolecules, xerophytes/halophytes, cell organelles (lysosome, chloroplast), photosynthesis. **Botany-heavy, almost zero zoology/human physiology this year.**

**Chemistry (10 Qs):** Colloids, isotopes, metal storage (Na, K, Li in oil), KNO3 uses, trade-name vs chemical-name matching (bleaching powder, baking soda, washing soda, Plaster of Paris), glass properties, atomic structure (proton/electron/neutron roles), bleaching agents, ozone layer chemistry.

**Physics (6 Qs):** Speed of sound in different media, heat transfer (conduction/convection/radiation), wave problems (frequency-wavelength-time numerical), rainbow formation (refraction+reflection), X-ray applications.

**History (16 Qs):** Ancient (Rigveda cereals, stupas/Kinjalkini, mesolithic rock art, Chola bronzes, Mauryan administration terms — Samahartri/Dauvarika/Akshapatala), Medieval (Shivaji's Ashta Pradhan, widow remarriage 18th century, Utpala dynasty Kashmir), Modern/Freedom Struggle (Lucknow Pact, Cripps Mission, Naval Mutiny, Cabinet Mission, Round Table Conference, Ambedkar's draft constitution terminology, Dalhousie's "cherry" quote on Awadh).

**Polity (16 Qs):** Flag Code of India, State Emblem, IPC/CrPC List classification, Article 371G (Mizoram), IT Act 2000, National Recruitment Agency/CET, SCO, FATF, UN voting procedures, Section 377 challengers (Naz Foundation), Citizenship Amendment Act exemptions.

**Geography (20 Qs):** Heaviest GK subject — Indian rivers (Barak, Ghaggar, Brahmaputra tributaries), Himalayan glaciers (Siachen, Zemu), Ganga basin states, peninsular rivers (Kabini→Godavari), Ramsar wetlands, Antarctica facts, ports (Singapore, Karwar, Dover), clouds, weathering, volcanoes, earthquake waves, pressure gradients/isobars, Hadley cells, soil-crop relations, Rhine river, mountain peaks in India.

**Economy (15 Qs):** Vostro accounts, fiscal multiplier, Long-Term Low Emissions Strategy, 12th Five Year Plan focus, Balance of Payments, Atmanirbhar Bharat, perfect competition equilibrium, Finance Commission tax-sharing, HDI components, demand-supply, inflation rate calculation, Rangarajan Committee poverty line, NIIFL funds, Pandit Deendayal Upadhyay Fund, Union Budget revenue expenditure.

**Current Affairs 2023–2024 (23 Qs):** ISRO missions (Chandrayaan-3, Aditya-L1), Mission Shakti (DRDO), presbyopia, Lagrange point L4, Vibrant Village Programme, BRICS HDI rankings, India-EFTA TEPA, India-Pakistan agreement chronology, Badminton Asia Championship 2024, NISHTHA programme, PM-JANMAN scheme, SAGAR-SETU/Sagar Manthan, Ghepang Ghat glacier, Bru-Reang/NLFT/TIPRA agreements, 96th Academy Awards, India's first transgender OPD, INS Tarmugli, COP28 resolutions, India-USA MoU (Railways Net Zero 2030).

## C.4 Sub-Topic Level Breakdown — English

**Grammar Sub-Topics:** Subject-Verb Agreement (Q11, 15, 75, 80), Tense forms (Q12, 13), Relative Pronouns (Q8, 14, 78), Quantifiers (Q64–66), Articles (Q67, 69, 70), Prepositions of time (Q61–63), Gerund/Infinitive/Participle (Q43), Modal verbs (Q99), Common collocation errors (Q77, 112–115), Active-Passive transformation (Q116–120).

**Vocabulary Sub-Topics:** Difficult/GS-level antonym words (equivocal, mutinous, belligerent, effusive, iniquitous), context-based fill-blanks (opulent, precipitates, excruciating), advanced synonyms (cantankerous, pernicious, verbose, resonant, doltish), abstract/academic meaning-words (Anarchism, Pristine, Enormity, Quotidian, Immolate, Deify, Raconteur, Emasculate, Perturbation).

**Sentence Construction Sub-Topics:** Single sentence jumbles — mostly GK/policy content (gender budgeting, NEP 2020, Amazon basin, Siddha medicine). Para-jumbles — **Current Affairs/Govt. Schemes based**: Atmanirbhar Bharat, SC/ST safeguards, Startup India, Livestock Census, Covid-19/ARHC, PTI, NSD, Gaganyaan, Youth Affairs Dept.

**Reading Comprehension Sub-Topics:** Passage I — literary/descriptive essay (walking tours, solitude philosophy). Passage II — academic/analytical essay (historiography, philosophical schools — Hegel, Marx, Comte, structuralism, postmodernism).

## C.5 Strategic Observations — English

1. **Grammar dominates everything** — 45 questions alone, maximum ROI area if time-limited.
2. **Vocabulary is GS/UPSC-level, not basic school-level** — words like "iniquitous," "raconteur," "emasculate" demand daily advanced word-list revision.
3. **Para-jumbles are Current Affairs flavored** — direct overlap between GK prep and English prep; strong GK makes these paragraphs easier to logically sequence.
4. **Idioms — limited, classic, repeatable set** ("watched pot never boils," "tilt at windmills," "play devil's advocate") — 100-150 common idioms cover this section well.
5. **Matching List (Q101–105)** — GK+vocabulary hybrid (animal sounds/groupings, sports terms) — low weight but easy marks if prepared.
6. **Single-word improvement questions test collocations**, not pure grammar rules (e.g., "centred on" vs "centred around").
7. **Sentence-combining tests logical connector relationships** (contrast/cause/condition) — although, because, though, nevertheless, whereas.

## D.2 My Observations — Across All Three Papers

1. **GK and English overlap meaningfully** — English para-jumbles are built on Current Affairs/Govt. Scheme content (Atmanirbhar Bharat, Gaganyaan, NEP, Startup India). Iska matlab: agar aap GK ka current affairs strong rakhte ho, to English ke 10 para-jumble questions bhi automatically easier ho jaate hain. **Yeh ek hidden synergy hai — GK aur English ko isolated subjects mat samjho.**

2. **Maths is "formula recall," English is "rule + vocabulary recall," GK is "fact recall + reasoning"** — teeno papers mein deep derivation/analysis kam, fast recognition aur application zyada chahiye. Speed-drilling (timed practice sets) is more valuable than slow conceptual study at this stage.

3. **Data Sufficiency (Maths, Q61–70) aur List Matching (English, Q101–105)** dono "predictable fixed blocks" hain — exact same position, same format, every paper. Yeh **guaranteed, low-effort-high-certainty marks** hain — inhe specifically practice karna chahiye kyunki format hamesha same rehta hai.

4. **Polity is getting more legally detailed** (specific Articles, Act sections, Citizenship Act exemptions) rather than just "structure of government" — basic civics is no longer enough.

5. **Economy is increasingly numerical/application-based** (inflation from price index, fiscal multiplier logic) — pure definitions won't suffice.

6. **English vocabulary is genuinely UPSC/CAT level**, not school-level — this is often underestimated by candidates switching from school-English prep to CDS-English prep.

7. **Total combined weightage check:** Across all 3 papers (340 total marks: 100+100+100... actually 340 questions total but check exact marks distribution per your scheme), the subjects that appear in **3+ different forms** (Current Affairs in GK + Current Affairs context in English jumbles; Geometry/Number System pure Maths) are the **highest-leverage study areas** — mastering these pays off in more than one paper.
